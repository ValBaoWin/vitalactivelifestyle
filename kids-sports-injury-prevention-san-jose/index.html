Build Good Movement Habits Early

Kids and teens are still growing, learning, and adapting. At VAL Chiropractic, Dr. Lam Nguyen evaluates how a young athlete moves, listens to the family's concerns, and offers practical guidance that fits the child's age, sport, and current ability.

Our goal is not to push heavier weight. It is to help young athletes move with control, understand good technique, and build confidence they can carry into sports and everyday activity.

Proper Lifting Starts With the Basics

  • Set up with balance and stable foot contact before starting the movement.
  • Keep the load close and use a comfortable, controlled range of motion.
  • Brace and breathe instead of rushing or holding the breath through a difficult lift.
  • Practice with little or no weight until the movement can be repeated with good form.
  • Increase gradually and stop when pain, fatigue, or loss of technique appears.
  • Use qualified supervision for resistance training and unfamiliar equipment.

Training & Technique Coaching

Learn proper lifting mechanics and understand the principles that support healthy muscle development—including technique, progressive resistance, consistency, nutrition, and recovery. Guidance is tailored to the athlete's mobility, experience, goals, and sport—not a cookie-cutter program or a random online video.

Dr. Nguyen can help identify where form begins to break down, explain why a movement may not feel right, and teach safer ways to squat, hinge, push, pull, brace, and carry. The focus is on building a strong foundation before adding more weight or intensity.

A Practical Injury-Prevention Mindset

No program can prevent every injury. However, young athletes can reduce avoidable risks by warming up, learning sport-specific technique, increasing workload gradually, taking recovery seriously, and speaking up when pain begins instead of trying to play through it.

What to Expect at a Visit

A visit may include a health and activity history, movement and mobility assessment, review of lifting or sport technique, and age-appropriate recommendations. A parent or guardian is included in the conversation so the plan and next steps are clear.

Important: sudden or severe pain, a suspected fracture or concussion, numbness, weakness, significant swelling, or difficulty bearing weight should be evaluated promptly by an appropriate medical professional. Chiropractic care does not replace pediatric or emergency medical care.

Common Questions

Kids & Sports FAQs

Is strength training appropriate for children and teens?

It may be appropriate when the young person can follow instructions and training is age-appropriate and supervised. Technique, control, and gradual progression should come before heavier weight. Families should consult their child's pediatrician when medical conditions or other concerns are present.

What does a youth movement visit include?

The visit may include a health and activity history, movement and mobility assessment, review of lifting or sport technique, and recommendations based on the findings.

Can all sports injuries be prevented?

No. No program can prevent every injury. Good technique, appropriate training load, recovery, and early attention to pain can help reduce avoidable risks.
