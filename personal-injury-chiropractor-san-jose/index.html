Clinical Care and Case Coordination Are Different Jobs

Your examination and treatment recommendations are based on your health history, physical findings, movement limitations, and response to care. They are not determined by an insurance claim or legal strategy.

Separately, the office keeps organized clinical records and can help coordinate authorized administrative requests related to a qualifying personal injury case. This distinction keeps the focus on appropriate care while making the paperwork easier to understand.

Important: VAL Chiropractic provides healthcare, not legal advice. Treatment is recommended only when supported by the clinical evaluation, and no outcome or settlement is guaranteed.

What the Office Can Coordinate

When appropriate and authorized by you, VAL Chiropractic can help keep the administrative side of care organized:

  • Clinical documentation — history, examination findings, care recommendations, visit dates, and progress
  • Record requests — release of appropriate records with a valid patient authorization
  • Billing information — responses to appropriate billing inquiries from authorized parties
  • Lien arrangements — review of lien-based care for qualifying personal injury cases
  • Case contacts — clear contact information for an attorney or applicable insurer when provided by the patient

What Lien-Based Care Means

A medical lien is a written payment arrangement that may allow qualifying patients to begin accepted care without paying the full balance at each visit. Payment may be deferred and handled from a future settlement. Acceptance is not automatic, and the terms should be reviewed before care begins.

A lien does not guarantee legal recovery or a particular health outcome. Questions about legal rights, liability, or settlement decisions should be directed to a qualified attorney.

What to Bring to Your Consultation

  1. Photo identification and your preferred contact information.
  2. Available medical records such as imaging or visit summaries, if you have them.
  3. Claim information and the applicable insurer contact, when available.
  4. Attorney information if you are already represented.

You can still call if you do not yet have every document. The office will explain which information is needed next.

Common Questions

Personal Injury FAQs

What is lien-based chiropractic care?

For qualifying personal injury cases, a lien may allow payment for accepted care to be deferred and handled from a future settlement. Eligibility and terms are reviewed before care begins.

Do I need an attorney before contacting VAL Chiropractic?

No. You may contact the office first for a clinical consultation and an explanation of available administrative options. VAL Chiropractic does not provide legal advice.

What records are maintained for a personal injury case?

The office maintains clinical records such as the patient history, examination findings, care plan, visit dates, and documented progress. Records are shared only with the patient's authorization or as otherwise permitted by law.

What should I bring to a personal injury consultation?

Bring photo identification, any available medical or imaging reports, claim information, and attorney contact information if you are represented. Do not delay calling if you do not yet have every document.
